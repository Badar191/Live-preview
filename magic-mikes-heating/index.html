Indoor air filtration

Start with a good filter box — it holds the filter tight and seals the door so the air cannot go around it. MERV 11 filters well without putting too much restriction on airflow. MERV 13 raises static pressure, so have a technician check static before you go up to one; too much restriction leads to early blower-motor failure. Smart filters are a good option, as long as you actually check the reminders.

What a Basic gas-furnace visit actually covers

One to two hours on site. Advanced adds the deep clean and a combustion analysis; Magic adds the parts that commonly fail. This list is theirs, not a summary of it.

  • Turn the furnace on to confirm it is operational
  • Clean the control board, wires, burner cabinet and the back of the blower motor
  • Check that wiring is tight and secure
  • Clean the flame sensor and record the micro-amps
  • Inspect the igniter and record its ohms
  • Inspect the capacitors and record the MFD reading
  • Clear drains, clean the p-trap, clear the vinyl tubing with nitrogen
  • Clear the pressure ports, pressure tubing and equalising tubing
  • Check the pressure switches
  • Check venting for integrity and safety
  • Replace the filter and date it
  • Test run for proper staging and operation
  • Take and record motor amp draws
  • Check and record temperature rise
  • Check and record CO in the home

Answers

Straight answers,
written by the techs

These are their own answers from their FAQ page — including the sizing numbers for our region here in Portland, Oregon.

Can I walk into an HVAC store and buy parts or equipment?

Generally, no. You cannot walk into just any HVAC equipment supplier and expect them to sell you anything, unless you are an HVAC specialty contractor with a business account set up.

That said, there is one DIY HVAC business here in Portland that we know of: Vinje and Sons Sheet Metal. They are highly recommended.

What exactly is HVAC?

It is an acronym for heating, ventilation and air conditioning. Sometimes there is an R after it, as in HVAC/R — that adds the commercial refrigeration side.

How long does HVAC equipment last?

On average, about 15 to 20 years. Units are being built with a 20-year life expectancy, though it varies depending on when it was made and who made it. The further back you look, the longer the systems lasted. We have come a long way from cast iron and brick, and that is why they say they don’t make them like they used to.

How often should I service my HVAC system?

Follow the manufacturer’s guidelines in order to keep warranties valid. Most of them recommend once per year.

Typically A/C maintenance is done May–June, and furnace maintenance September–October.

What size A/C unit do I need for my home?

You can get a rough estimate with these rules of thumb. This is for our region here in Portland, Oregon, using conventional equipment.

  • 1.5 tons600–1,000 sq ft
  • 2.0 tons1,001–1,300 sq ft
  • 2.5 tons1,301–1,600 sq ft
  • 3.0 tons1,601–1,900 sq ft
  • 3.5 tons1,901–2,200 sq ft
  • 4.0 tons2,201–2,600 sq ft
  • 5.0 tons2,601–3,200 sq ft

Typically you don’t count the garage unless it is finished and supplied with conditioned air. Newer homes that might be regarded as “tight” may need slightly fewer BTUs and a bit more fresh air. These are general guidelines; for better accuracy, a load calculation can be done.

For mini-splits you go off room size. The most common sizes:

  • 3/4 ton350–400 sq ft — 9,000 BTUh
  • 1.0 ton450–550 sq ft — 12,000 BTUh
  • 1.5 tons700–1,000 sq ft — 18,000 BTUh
  • 2.0 tonsup to 1,500 sq ft — 24,000 BTUh
What can I do to lower my energy bills?

There are numerous ways to lower a cooling bill in the summer months.

  • Turn off lights and TVs when they are not in use.
  • Keep the windows closed while the day heats up, especially at the hottest part of it. Open them at night to cool the structure down, then close them again in the morning.
  • Use blackout curtains on all large windows.
  • Keep the furnace fan on, even if you don’t have A/C.
  • Replace the filters as needed.
  • Turn off and unplug unused electrical devices — even the charging blocks.
  • Check for gaps and drafts at exterior doors and windows, seal them, and consider a draft preventer at the bottom of the door.
  • If you have a basement, make sure air from it is circulating through the house.
  • If you have A/C, maintenance is the best thing you can do for it.
  • Cook outside where you can. A barbecue is a great way to handle the heat, and workouts are better outdoors too.

The idea here is BTU infiltration. There is only heat and the absence of heat — we are not creating cold with an air conditioner, we are moving BTUs out of the building envelope. Slow the heat coming in and the A/C runs less, which saves you money.

Why is my house still warm with the air conditioner on?

It could be excessive heat infiltration — lots of guests, and the exterior doors opening constantly. But if everything is normal at home and the system is not keeping up the way it usually does, that can be a sign of poor performance.

What can I check before calling for service?
  • Check the filter. If it is dirty, replace it, then see whether the system starts cooling better.
  • Check for ice. If the filter was really dirty there may be ice on the system, usually at the indoor coil. Replace the filter and allow enough time for all the ice to melt completely before starting up again.
  • Check the breaker. If it is tripped the unit will not run. Reset it once only — if it trips instantly again, you need a repairman.
  • Check the thermostat batteries and replace them if needed.
  • Make sure the thermostat is actually calling for cooling.
  • Look for obvious obstructions, such as a branch that has fallen into the unit.
  • Check whether the indoor fan is spinning.
  • Take note of any loud noises or burning smells.

These notes help the technician get to the heart of the problem.
